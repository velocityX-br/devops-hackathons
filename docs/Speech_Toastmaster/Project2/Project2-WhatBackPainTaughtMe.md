# What Back Pain Taught Me

**Toastmasters Project 2 — Organize Your Speech**  
**Time:** 5–7 minutes · **~650 words**  
**Primary outline:** Topical（三个 lessons）  
**Narrative spine:** Chronological（忽略信号 → 严重周末 → 之后的改变）  
**Speech objective:** Inform — what back pain taught *me*（I-statements；不说教）

---

## 1. Clean Script（排练用 · 无长注释）

Good morning, Toastmasters and guests.

Last weekend, I woke up, tried to get out of bed—and froze. A sharp pain shot through my lower back. For a few seconds, I just sat there, wondering how someone who sits in meetings all week could suddenly feel like he had run a marathon he never signed up for.

I had felt mild back discomfort before. It usually came, stayed a little while, and left. That weekend was different. So this morning, I want to share three things that weekend taught me.

**First, I had been ignoring quiet signals for a long time.**

Looking back, the warning signs were everywhere. After long coding sessions, I would stand up stiff. After flights, my lower back would ache for a day or two. I told myself the same story many of us tell: “I’m just tired. It will pass.” And for years, it did pass—until it didn’t.

Here is what that means for me: at work, when a service shows a small error, I investigate. When my back whispered, I muted it. That weekend was not a sudden accident. It was a bill that had been collecting interest.

**Second, I tried to buy my way out of the problem.**

As a result, the Monday after that painful weekend, I did what a modern professional does: I opened a shopping app. Standing desk. Lumbar cushion. Maybe even one of those fancy race-car chairs. I told myself, “If I upgrade the equipment, I upgrade the body.”

To illustrate how wrong that thinking was—my first standing desk arrived quickly. I raised it, stood tall, typed one email—and the whole thing started to wobble like a boat in mild wind. I felt ridiculous: a grown man, carefully balancing on furniture that seemed unsure about its own career. My colleagues laughed. I laughed too. But under the joke, I felt something else. Buying the desk was easy. Changing how I worked was harder.

More importantly, the desk did not fix me. It only gave me a new place to stand while I kept the same old habits: long hours, few breaks, and “just one more task.”

**Third, I learned that rest is also a skill.**

This was the hardest lesson for me. In our world—especially in fast-paced Shanghai—I used to think a good day meant a full calendar and a cleared inbox. Pain forced me to ask a different question: What if a good day also includes space?

So I started small. Not a dramatic life reset. Just tiny experiments. I set a timer to stand up. I took short walks between meetings, sometimes only to the pantry and back. I stopped treating lunch as optional. Some days I still forget. Some days I still push too hard. But now, when my back sends a quiet signal, I try not to mute it.

I am not here to tell you what to do with your body. I am here to tell you what mine taught me when I finally listened. Let me leave you with those three lessons again.

First, quiet signals matter—ignoring them does not make them disappear.  
Second, new tools help, but they cannot replace new habits.  
Third, rest is not laziness; for me, it became a form of maintenance.

Finally, I am learning to treat balance less like a slogan and more like a daily practice. For me, it starts with standing up before pain arrives, closing the laptop for lunch, and noticing a quiet signal before it becomes loud.

I still sit through long meetings, and some mornings I still rush. But I no longer treat that frozen moment as a one-time accident. That weekend, my back stopped whispering and started shouting—and I do not want to wait for it again.

Back pain did not make me perfect. It made sure that when my body talks, I finally started listening.

Thank you, Mister / Madam Toastmaster.

## 4. Delivery Notes


| Move                | Cue                                     | Transition used                | Guidelines 位置             |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Opening → Body      | “…three things that weekend taught me.” | Then **First…**                | intro → body              |
| Main1 → Sub/Support | After stating First                     | **Looking back…**              | main → sub / support      |
| Support1 → Main2    | After “collecting interest”             | **Second…** / **As a result…** | support → next main       |
| Main2 → Support     | After shopping list                     | **To illustrate…**             | sub → support             |
| Inside Main2        | After wobble story                      | **More importantly…**          | deepen same point         |
| Support2 → Main3    | After old habits                        | **Third…**                     | support → next main       |
| Body → Close        | After “when I finally listened”         | **Let me leave you…**          | last support → conclusion |


**Memorize:** opening (bed freeze → three things) + closing (summary → bed callback → “started listening”).  
**Gestures:** freeze；desk wobble；open palm on “quiet signals”.  
**Pace:** smile on wobble story；slow down on final three-line recap.  
**Fix rehearsed:** 统一用 **Good morning**（已去掉 Tonight）。
